# Design principles

These skills grew from one learner's college workflow. They are practical defaults for that context, not a policy for other courses or learners. Developing this repository is not an assignment; the rules below concern using its skills on assessed work.

- **Start with the current brief.** Read the assessment instructions and workspace notes before giving advice. Apply the rules for that assignment rather than assuming an earlier brief governs it.
- **Keep the learner's work theirs.** Prefer feedback, source notes, evidence checks, and clearly labelled non-submittable prompts over ready-to-submit assessed prose. Edit a learner's draft only within the scope they authorize.
- **Separate claims from evidence.** Distinguish a learner-reported submission or instructor exception from a verified brief, receipt, screenshot, or export. State what was checked and what remains unverified.
- **Check the actual deliverable.** Map brief requirements to evidence in the current report, not just loose screenshots. Compare the editable source with its PDF/DOCX export and inspect rendered pages before claiming an image, correction, or layout survived export.
- **Preserve the record.** Keep submitted files, original evidence, editable sources, and protected backups intact. Archive superseded drafts without treating them as disposable.
- **Disclose actual assistance.** Record what the agent reviewed, created, or changed; do not imply that the learner approved or submitted something without confirmation.
- **Adapt presentation to the task.** Short, screenshot-led feedback and separate planning scaffolds suit this learner's practical assignments. They are preferences, not universal requirements.

These principles are distilled from working preferences and repeated project lessons, not copied from private `SOUL.md`, `USER.md`, or `MEMORY.md`. Do not publish personal identifiers, machine paths, live assignment status, or one-off course exceptions as general guidance.
